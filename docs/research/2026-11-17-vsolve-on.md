# The one solve's heights in the plans: BNE_VSOLVE=on (2026-11-17)

This is the RFC's step 3 (docs/VERTICAL_SOLVER_RFC.md): opt-in. With `BNE_VSOLVE=on`, a cell's ground plans
take the solve's heights, unless the solve fell back to the passes'. The passes still run first, because the
caps and floors come from their heights. The plans are cached under their own format (`plancache.plans_format`
gains ".vs1"), so they never mix with the passes' plans. All the numbers are in `2026-11-17-vsolve-on.json` (one run of each mode: "shadow", "on").

    python tools/build_native.py --fast     # earthfast 373 (the committed modules are 372)
    BNE_VSOLVE=on BNE_REVISIT=off BNGE_PLANCACHE=off python tools/grade_metrics.py --tiles 1 --out build/on.json

It ran on a local development build of the kernel: 8 places.

## A dip rang past its ramp

In on mode, three of `test_grading`'s underpass cases failed. A road under a motorway bridge dipped as it
should, then came back 0.16 m over its ground past the ramp, and 0.31 m at its end. With bending and a hold to
the profile alone, the solve behaves like a beam on a spring bed, and its response to a dip rings.

- A tension term set to its critical value (`length` = 2 sqrt(lam × hold), no oscillation) stopped the ringing.
  But it left the ramp's foot 0.3 to 1.1 m low, which is worse.
- Now, where the underpass rule lowered a road, the solve caps that road at its height before the rule, there and
  a spread (`vsolve.SPREAD_M`) either side. These are the soft clearance rows the caps under decks already use.
  `test_vsolve`'s new case checks it: it fails at 10.31 m without the cap.

Two cases still differ from the passes in on mode, by 0.11 and 0.2 m. The solve's ramp out of a dip is longer
than the rule's, so a motorway 250 m from its bridge is 0.11 m under its ground. A street's dip spreads 0.2 m past
the junction the rule stops it at. These are the solve's spread, not errors, but the tests ask for the passes'
shape. They pass in the default mode.

## Tokyo's decks moved, and why

The first measure of on mode gave the shadow's ground roads everywhere, but Tokyo had 139 low decks against the
passes' 72, and 18 roads through them against 6. At 35.63, 139.65 (the Shuto expressway's viaducts, 15 cells),
44 of 58 decks moved by more than 0.1 m between off and on, and whole chains by up to 11 m.

- A deck chain (`roads.BridgeProfiles`) is solved on the graded ground: the ground along its lines, the roads
  it crosses, and its ends. In on mode, those come from the solve's plans.
- Replayed alone with both modes' inputs, the 76-part chain of `5506483f…` moved 24.7 m. The roads it crosses
  explain 0.27 m of that. The ground along its lines explains all of it, and a bisection found one point:
  35.65821, 139.70695, 26.69 m off and 28.76 m on.
- There, the solve had raised a junction 2.7 m (26.74 → 29.48 m). That eased a ramp coming down to it from a
  deck's end. Its embankment lifted the ground under the viaduct. From that point the chain's solve (one path
  after another, each from the nodes solved before) carried the change to every part.
- No cap held the junction, for two reasons. Its road is 6.9 m from the deck's line, more than the deck's half
  width. And it lay by a joint between two of the viaduct's parts, which counted as an abutment (no cap within
  `APPROACH_MIN_CROSS_M` of a deck's end).

A road is now capped wherever its surface is under the deck: within the deck's half width plus its own. A deck's
end that another deck goes on from is a joint, not an abutment. `test_vsolve`'s new `CapsTest` covers both.
At 35.63, 139.65 in on mode, low decks went 207 → 78 and roads through them 29 → 13. The shadow solve has 78 and
13, the passes 81 and 13.

The chains' own sensitivity, 2 m of ground moving a chain 25 m, is still there. It belongs to the deck solve, not
to this one. It is listed under "Next".

## Lagos' 4 decks: the measure's, not the decks'

Lagos had 4 more low decks in on mode than in the shadow (39 against 35). They were two roads, `31a01f1a…` and
`94052fb4…`, each under a pair of parallel decks. Their heights were the same in both modes. The decks were
0.4 to 0.7 m lower in on mode.

- Replayed alone, the two chains moved with the roads they cross (`required` in `BridgeProfiles._solve`), not with
  the ground along their lines. The solve lowers those roads more than the passes' dip, and a deck held by its
  clearance over them comes down with them. Every one still cleared them by its own clearance or more.
- That clearance is `roads.deck_road_clear(depth)`: the headroom, its margin and the deck's own slab and girders,
  1.44 m on these short spans. The grading's rules use it too. The measure used `roads.DECK_ROAD_CLEAR`, a long
  span's 2.2 m depth, so a thin deck 6.4 m over its road counted as low.

`grademetrics.clearance` now takes each deck's depth (`ChainView.depth_of`) and measures against the clearance
the deck was solved with. `test_grademetrics` covers a thin deck. With it, Lagos has 17 / 16 / 16 low decks and
Tokyo 50 / 49 / 49, and the low decks left are those short of their own clearance. The other columns did not move.
