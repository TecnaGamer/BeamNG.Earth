# The one solve's heights in the plans: BNE_VSOLVE=on (2026-11-17)

This is the RFC's step 3 (docs/VERTICAL_SOLVER_RFC.md): opt-in. With `BNE_VSOLVE=on`, a cell's ground plans
take the solve's heights, unless the solve fell back to the passes'. The passes still run first, because the
caps and floors come from their heights. The plans are cached under their own format (`plancache.plans_format`
gains ".vs1"), so they never mix with the passes' plans. All the numbers are in `2026-11-17-vsolve-on.json`.

    python tools/build_native.py --fast     # earthfast 373 (the committed modules are 372)
    BNE_VSOLVE=on BNE_REVISIT=off BNGE_PLANCACHE=off python tools/grade_metrics.py --tiles 1 --out build/on.json

It ran on a local development build of the kernel: 8 places.

## A dip rang past its ramp

In on mode, three of `test_grading`'s underpass cases failed. A road under a motorway bridge dipped as it
should, then came back 0.16 m over its ground past the ramp, and 0.31 m at its end. With bending and a hold to
the profile alone, the solve behaves like a beam on a spring bed, and its response to a dip rings.

- A tension term set to its critical value (`length` = 2 sqrt(lam × hold), no oscillation) stopped the ringing.
  But it left the ramp's foot 0.3 to 1.1 m low, which is worse.
- Now, where the underpass rule lowered a road, the solve caps that road at its height before the rule, there and
  a spread (`vsolve.SPREAD_M`) either side. These are the soft clearance rows the caps under decks already use.
  `test_vsolve`'s new case checks it: it fails at 10.31 m without the cap.

Two cases still differ from the passes in on mode, by 0.11 and 0.2 m. The solve's ramp out of a dip is longer
than the rule's, so a motorway 250 m from its bridge is 0.11 m under its ground. A street's dip spreads 0.2 m past
the junction the rule stops it at. These are the solve's spread, not errors, but the tests ask for the passes'
shape. They pass in the default mode.

## What is not done: Tokyo's decks move

Only Tokyo's decks get worse. In on mode they have twice the passes' low decks, and three times as many roads
through them. The ground roads are the same as in the shadow solve, and those roads measured against the
passes' decks give 68 low decks. So the decks themselves change.

At 35.63, 139.65 (Shuto expressway viaducts, 15 cells), each deck's heights were dumped in both modes. 44 of 58
decks moved by more than 0.1 m. Whole chains moved by up to 11 m, at their ends too: one chain's ends went from
30.9 and 30.0 m to 42.6 and 40.8 m, over 16 m of ground. A 4.4 km viaduct (`5506483f…`) came down 11 m in its
middle. Low decks there went 81 → 207 and roads through them 13 → 29.

The deck chains (`roads.BridgeProfiles`, through `blockworker._chain_store`) are solved on the physics ground.
That ground is the graded one, so it reads the plans: their heights where a road crosses under a deck, and at a
deck's ends. In on mode, those are the solve's heights. A change of a few centimetres in the roads cannot move a
chain 11 m, so something in the chains' solve switches. Two candidates have not been checked:

- a clearance or landing case that switches on the ground read at one crossing;
- the order in which chains and cells get solved, which changes with on mode's longer cells.

Until that is understood, on mode is not for use where there are decks over decks, as in Tokyo. The default
stays off.
