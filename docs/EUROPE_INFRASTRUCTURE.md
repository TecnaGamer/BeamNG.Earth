# European infrastructure profiles — v4

This is a representative scenery set, based on the user's comparison maps and
pole/delineator photographs. It is not an exhaustive national standards catalogue.
Regional and historic variants, multilingual layouts, exact sign fonts and every
pole configuration are not reproduced. Photographs are references, not assets.
Country selection comes from coordinates, not from visual appearance or airports.

The 56 codes below include dependencies and microstates, not 56 sovereign states.
Russia and Turkey are covered across their full source country geometry. Québec
is a separate STOP-label exception; Canadian poles and sidewalks stay stock.
Unknown countries and mode `off` retain the public behaviour.

Chevron column is arrow/background. Shared marker/pole family IDs refer to the
procedural builders. The town style is the selected representative nameplate or
built-up sign, never a new instruction to the AI or speed-limit system.

## Coverage and interpretation

France keeps surveyed OSM boards and its prior fallback. The other 55 codes use
Overture locality points and sustained built-up transitions. Placement can miss
an approach, choose the wrong nearby settlement, or overlap vegetation; these
are not legal/agglomeration boundaries or a real sign inventory. Residential
streets are excluded from estimated entrances. No motorway town-name boards.
Blue Finnish/Norwegian/Swedish/Baltic nameplates do not stand in for every built-up
area sign. Poland's green nameplates do not replace the separate D-42 sign.
British boards vary locally. Their profiles intentionally have no invented
crossed-out reverse where the chosen nameplate convention does not call for one.
CH/LI select blue on primary/secondary roads, white elsewhere. Romania's yellow
speed header is added only when the mapped road supplies a speed; it does not
change that speed. Turkey retains dotted uppercase İ in names.

Supplied photos guide representative poles; family mix is a deterministic
scenery choice, not measured national percentages. Less-documented microstates,
dependencies and AM/AZ/MD/RU/BY reuse nearby families as provisional defaults.
Those should receive local review before any upstream claim of full accuracy.
GE has some supplied pole references. All countries still have a working profile.

France J6 retains the v3 sparse 8% trial and 90 m spacing. Delineator deployment
now has separate country policies: Germany also equips short rural features and
tertiary/unclassified roads at 50 m, Austria uses 33 m, Denmark/Poland 100 m.
Other sourced profiles distinguish road classes, speed, lighting and local bend
markers. See DELINEATOR_RESEARCH.md for the complete implementation table,
sources, historical references and explicit provisional countries. Unverified
countries retain the v3 trial (35% of long primary/secondary features, 90 m).
Neither percentage is a measured national prevalence. No generated position is
claimed to be a surveyed real delineator.
All delineators are noncolliding. New pole collision hulls are simplified.
