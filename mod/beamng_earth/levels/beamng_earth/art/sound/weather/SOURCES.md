# Weather sounds (thunder and rain): generated, no third-party audio

Every file here was generated by BeamNG.Earth's own synthesiser, `native/weathersynth` (Rust, MIT, like the rest of
the repository), at commit `caa4b44d4` (agent branch `worktree-agent-aa7211bf2fd0acdb7`, 2026-11-25). No recordings,
samples or other third-party audio are used. The only outside code is the `libm` crate (=0.2.16, pure-Rust maths,
already in earthfast) and ffmpeg's libvorbis encoder (ffmpeg n9.0.2) for the Ogg files. These files replace the CC0
Freesound recordings (the user, 2026-11-25: "All of these have background noise. Also could you look into procedurally
generated sounds?").

## How to rebuild them

```
cd native/weathersynth && cargo build --release --offline
weathersynth set --out OUT1 --set 1 --only rain --ogg        # all rain loops (seed 101)
weathersynth set --out OUT1 --set 1 --only thunder --ogg     # thunder seeds 101, 102
weathersynth set --out OUT2 --set 2 --only thunder --ogg     # thunder seeds 201, 202
weathersynth set --out OUT3 --set 3 --only thunder --ogg     # thunder seeds 301, 302
```

`set --set K` renders thunder variant v of every class with seed 100 K + v, and every rain loop with seed 100 K + 1.
It writes a mono 44.1 kHz float WAV, then encodes it with
`ffmpeg -i X.wav -ac 1 -ar 44100 -c:a libvorbis -q:a Q -map_metadata -1 -fflags +bitexact X.ogg` (Q = 5 for strike, 4
for everything else). It decodes each Ogg, measures the true peak, and renders again with a lower limiter ceiling if
Vorbis overshot the class's target. The renders are deterministic: libm maths, one thread, a fixed summation order.
This folder was rebuilt that way, and all 24 thunder files came out byte-identical to the earlier renders the user
listened to (`_work/thunder_compare.txt`). The script is `_work/render_final.sh`.

- **Thunder:** the loudness is that of the loudest 3 s (EBU R128 short-term). Each file starts where the sound
  becomes audible; the game delays it by the strike's distance after the flash.
- **Rain:** 20 s seamless loops (written round a circular buffer), integrated loudness over the loop played twice.
  The three intensities are stepped on purpose: light -30, medium -24, heavy -17 LUFS. The mod's own volume curve
  (weather.lua `RAIN_CURVE` × `RAIN_GAIN`) adds about 7.5 dB on top, light to heavy.

## How they are made (short; details in native/weathersynth/src)

- **Thunder:**
  - Channel: a random tortuous lightning channel: macro segments with ~16° turns, meso steps, fractal
    micro-tortuosity, branches, in-cloud channels, and 1-5 return strokes moved by the wind.
  - Source: every piece radiates an N-wave sized by Few's relaxation radius (1969), with weak-shock lengthening.
  - Summing: the pieces are summed with their propagation delay (Ribner & Roy 1982).
  - Propagation: ISO 9613-1 air absorption as minimum-phase filters per 1 % distance bin, a soft-ground reflection,
    terrain echo clusters and a diffuse tail.
  - Spectrum: an empirical +10 dB/oct source tilt above 150 Hz, calibrated to recorded thunder.
  - Level: a true-peak limiter to each class's loudness.
- **Rain:**
  - Drop sizes: Joss drizzle (light), Marshall-Palmer (medium) and Joss thunderstorm (heavy), at terminal speed.
  - Impacts: amplitude ~ D^1.5·v^2.5 (Guo & Ffowcs Williams 1991).
  - Surfaces: road, puddles (Minnaert bubbles, van den Doel 2005), grass, leaves and metal, in the tone the user
    picked ("D": fewer and lower bubbles, splash an octave lower with a 200-800 Hz thud, -4 dB above 4 kHz, leaves
    and drips a fifth lower, damped metal).
  - The wash: the drops' mean spectrum at their physical power (after Liu, Cheng & Tong 2019), with ground-effect
    attenuation on the far rings.
  - Extras: drip points and canopy drips, near objects, standing water and runoff, depending on intensity.
  - Car roof: the drops drive the roof panel's plate modes and the windscreen's, heard through the headliner.
- No third-party audio, samples or recordings are used anywhere.
